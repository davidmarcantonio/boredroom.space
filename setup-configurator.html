Set up with Apple Configurator

For the one-person IT shop with a handful of iPads and a Mac. You get supervised devices, app installs without an Apple ID on the iPad, and a kiosk lock that survives reboots. No MDM subscription.

Print this page with ⌘P. Links print with their addresses.

Before you start

Four prerequisites, each done once. The slowest is Apple Business Manager approval, so start with that. Nothing below touches an iPad.

A. Apple Business Manager

  1. Enroll at business.apple.com (Apple now calls the service "Apple Business"; MDM consoles still say Apple Business Manager). Free. Needs your D-U-N-S number matching your legal name and address, and a verification phone call to a second person at your company (not whoever fills in the form). Apple doesn't promise a turnaround; answer their call promptly.
  2. Once approved: accept the Apps and Books terms when prompted, and add a payment method (Settings › Payment Information). Required even for free apps.
  3. Apps and Books › search Bored Room › pick the location (the default is fine; remember which) › quantity = number of iPads › Buy. 99¢ per copy.

B. Google: a service account shared with every room

Configured iPads have no person signed in, so they read the rooms through a free Google service account. Follow Set up a Google service account (about 20 minutes, once). You come back with:

  • the key file (a .json)
  • every room shared with the service account's email
  • a list of room names and calendar IDs

C. Apple Configurator with an organization

  1. Install Apple Configurator from the Mac App Store (free). Have a USB-C cable; a direct port works better than a hub.
  2. Configurator › Settings (⌘,) › Organizations › +. If it offers to sign in to Apple Business Manager, you can sign in or skip; this sign-in is only for adding devices to Business Manager, not for app licenses. Enter the organization name; the rest is optional. Choose Generate a new supervision identity › Done.
  3. Still in Organizations: select yours › the action menu (gear or …) › Export Supervision Identity › choose a passphrase. You get an encrypted .p12 file. Save it and the passphrase with your other IT passwords. (A replacement Mac gets it back via Organizations › … › Import Organization.)
  4. Menu bar › Account › Sign In with your Business Manager account › enter the six-digit code › pick the same location you bought the licenses for. This is the sign-in that lets Configurator install the app with no Apple ID on the iPad. Your account needs the Administrator or Content Manager role.

The supervision identity is a certificate Configurator makes for you. Nothing to request or install beforehand. It's what ties these iPads to this Mac: starting or stopping Single App Mode later, or updating the app, needs the same identity. If it's lost, the only way back in is to erase and prepare the iPad again. That's why step 3 exports it.

D. iPads you can erase

  • Any iPad that runs iPadOS 17 or newer.
  • Supervision wipes the device. Back up anything on used iPads; new ones can stay on the Hello screen.
  • A power source at each mounting spot. The app keeps the screen on, which needs power.

Checklist

  • Business Manager approved; N copies of Bored Room bought and assigned (A)
  • Service account key file in hand; every room shared with it; room names and calendar IDs listed (B)
  • Configurator installed; organization created with a generated supervision identity; identity exported; Account › Sign In done for the right location (C)
  • iPads, cable, power at the mount, backups done where needed (D)

Prepare each iPad

Do the first one end to end, then repeat. About five minutes each once you've done one.

  1. Plug the iPad into the Mac. In Configurator, select it › Prepare.
  2. First screen: Manual Configuration, with four checkboxes under it. Tick Supervise devices. Leave "Add to Apple Business Manager" and "Enable Shared iPad" unticked. The remaining one is the next step.
  3. Allow devices to pair with other computers: read this before choosing. Unchecked means that from now on, only a Mac holding this organization's supervision identity (the one you generated and exported in C) can connect to the iPad at all: no other Mac or PC will be able to see it, back it up, update the app, or stop Single App Mode. If this Mac dies and you never exported the identity, the only way back in is to erase the iPad with Recovery Mode and start over. That is the more locked-down choice and fine for a wall sign, as long as the exported identity is somewhere safe. Checked lets any computer pair with the iPad, which is more forgiving and slightly less secure. Next.
  4. Device management: Do not enroll in Device Management. Next.
  5. If a Managed Apple Account sign-in screen appears, click Skip.
  6. Organization: the one from C. Next.
  7. Setup Assistant: choose to skip all the panes (the option is worded as showing none of the steps). This skips the Apple ID, Siri, and Screen Time prompts on the iPad. Next.
  8. If a Wi-Fi or network profile screen appears, you can continue without one; the iPad gets its internet over the cable from the Mac during preparation. Prepare.
  9. The iPad erases, restarts, and lands on the Home screen with no Apple ID. Connect it to Wi-Fi (Settings › Wi-Fi).
  10. On the iPad: Settings › Accessibility › Display & Text Size › Auto-Brightness off (so the light sensor doesn't undo the after-hours dimming), and Settings › Display & Brightness › Auto-Lock › Never.
  11. Back in Configurator, select the iPad › Add › Apps › pick Bored Room from your Business Manager apps (the number beside it is licenses left) › Add Apps. It installs without an Apple ID. If the list is empty, Account › Sign In (C) is missing or pointed at the wrong location.

Set up the app

  1. Get the service account key onto the iPad. Easiest from the same Mac: AirDrop the .json; accept it on the iPad and it lands in Files. (Alternative: Configurator › Add › Documents puts it in a chosen app's folder; Files is simpler.)
  2. Open Bored Room › Use a service account › Choose key file. Files opens: Downloads in the sidebar if you AirDropped it (if the iPad asked which app should open it, you chose Files), or the app's folder if you used Configurator › Add › Documents. Tap the .json.
  3. If this iPad's room isn't in the list, paste its calendar ID (from your list in B) into Add a room by its calendar ID › Add. Only the first iPad for each room needs this; the others see it. Easiest way to get the ID onto the iPad: AirDrop a text file with the IDs from the same Mac, open it in Files, copy the one you need.
  4. Pick this iPad's room › Continue › confirm the name › Continue › set a 4-digit PIN. Same PIN on every iPad; store it in your password manager next to the key file and the exported supervision identity.
  5. Pick the days and hours the board is on (Mon–Fri 7–5 by default, or Always on) › Finish. Outside those hours it's black at minimum backlight and wakes on touch.
  6. Optional: hold the corner tab › PIN › Settings to change the font, background, titles, booking, or hours.

Lock it

  1. With Bored Room open on the iPad and the iPad still connected, in Configurator select it › Actions › Advanced › Start Single App Mode.
  2. Choose Bored Room. Click Options: tick Disable Volume Buttons, Disable Sleep/Wake Button (the app manages the screen; this stops passers-by turning it off) and Disable Auto-Lock. Leave Disable Touch unticked. Apply › Select App.
  3. The iPad is now locked to the board, through reboots and power cuts. To get out: connect it to the Mac › Actions › Advanced › Stop Single App Mode.

Settings still work inside Single App Mode. The PIN-protected settings are part of the app, so you can change the room or the look from the wall without unlocking the device.

Mount it

  • Power is non-negotiable. A mount with cable routing, or a cable to a nearby outlet.
  • Landscape is the usual orientation for a door sign; the board handles either.

Ongoing

  • App updates: with no Apple ID and no MDM, nothing updates the app on its own. Reconnect to the Mac, select the iPad, toolbar Update › Apps only (needs Account › Sign In active). Or move to an MDM when the count justifies it.
  • Adding a room: share it with the service account and note its calendar ID (service account guide, steps 3 and 4). On an iPad: hold the corner › PIN › Settings › Calendar › paste the ID into Add a room by its calendar ID. Every other iPad sees it from then on.
  • Rotating the key: Cloud Console › the service account › Keys › add a new key, delete the old one. Each iPad: hold the corner › PIN › Settings › Remove service account, then load the new file.
  • Forgotten PIN: hold the corner › Forgot PIN? › choose the key file again › new PIN. The iPad stays on the wall. (If the Files picker won't open under Single App Mode, stop Single App Mode from Configurator first.)

If something's off

  • Configurator can't see my Business Manager apps. You're not signed in under Account › Sign In, or you picked a different location there than the one you bought the licenses in. Window › Volume Purchasing Assignments shows what Configurator can see.
  • "Prepare" fails partway. Usually the cable or a USB hub. Use a direct port and Apple's cable.
  • No rooms appear in the app. Expected the first time: add the room by its calendar ID. If Add says Google won't open the calendar, the room isn't shared with the service account yet, or the sharing setting hasn't applied (it can take up to 24 hours).
  • The board is right but Single App Mode won't start. The iPad must be supervised by this Mac's organization. If it was supervised elsewhere, re-prepare it.
