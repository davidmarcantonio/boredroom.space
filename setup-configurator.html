Set up with Apple Configurator

For the one-person IT shop with a handful of iPads and a Mac. You get supervised devices, app installs without an Apple ID on the iPad, and a kiosk lock that survives reboots. No MDM subscription.

Print this page with ⌘P. Links print with their addresses.

What you need

  • A Mac with Apple Configurator (free, Mac App Store) and a USB-C cable.
  • An Apple Business Manager account (free). Takes a day or two to approve if you don't have one; it needs your D-U-N-S number.
  • Google Workspace admin access for the one-time Google steps.
  • iPads you're willing to erase. Supervision wipes them.

Part 1: Google (once)

Create a free service account, allow room sharing with it, share each room, download its JSON key. The by-hand guide walks through every click; the short version:

  1. Cloud Console: new project › enable Google Calendar API › IAM & Admin › Service Accounts › create one (no roles) › Keys › Add key › JSON. Keep the file and the account's email.
  2. Admin console: Apps › Google Workspace › Calendar › Sharing settings › External sharing options for secondary calendars › "Share all information, but outsiders cannot change calendars" (or "…can change" for booking from the door).
  3. Google Calendar: each room › Settings and sharing › Share with specific people › the service account's email › See all event details (or Make changes to events for booking).

Part 2: buy the app in Apple Business Manager

  1. business.apple.com › Apps and Books › search Bored Room › quantity = number of iPads › Get. It's 99¢ per copy, billed to the payment method on the account.
  2. Still in Apps and Books: assign the licenses to the location your Configurator will use (the default location is fine).
  3. Apple Configurator › Settings (⌘,) › Organizations › add your organization and sign in with your Business Manager account. This links Configurator to your licenses and creates a supervision identity.

Part 3: prepare each iPad

Do the first one end to end, then repeat. About five minutes each once you've done one.

  1. Plug the iPad into the Mac. If it's brand new, leave it on the Hello screen. If it's been used, it will be erased in the next step; back up anything you care about.
  2. In Configurator, select the iPad › Prepare.
  3. Configuration: Manual. Next.
  4. Enroll in MDM: Do not enroll. Next.
  5. Supervise devices: checked. Allow devices to pair with other computers: your call (unchecked is more locked down). Next.
  6. Organization: pick yours. Next.
  7. Setup Assistant: Don't show any of these steps. This skips the Apple ID, Siri, and Screen Time prompts on the iPad. Prepare.
  8. The iPad erases, restarts, and lands on the Home screen with no Apple ID. Connect it to Wi-Fi (Settings › Wi-Fi).
  9. Back in Configurator, select the iPad › Add › Apps › pick Bored Room from your Business Manager apps › Add. It installs without an Apple ID.

Part 4: set up the app

  1. Get the service account key onto the iPad. Easiest from the same Mac: AirDrop the .json to the iPad; accept it and it lands in Files. (Alternative: Configurator › Add › Documents puts it in a chosen app's folder; Files is simpler.)
  2. Open Bored Room › Use a service account › Choose key file › pick the .json.
  3. Pick this iPad's room › Continue › confirm the name › Continue › set a 4-digit PIN.
  4. Optional: hold the corner tab › PIN › Settings to change the font, background, titles, or booking.

Part 5: lock it

  1. With Bored Room open on the iPad and the iPad still connected, in Configurator select it › Actions › Advanced › Start Single App Mode.
  2. Choose Bored Room. Click Options and consider: Touch: on. Volume buttons: off. Sleep/Wake button: off (the app keeps the screen on; this stops passers-by turning it off). Auto-Lock: Never. Select App.
  3. The iPad is now locked to the board, through reboots and power cuts. To get out: connect it to the Mac › Actions › Advanced › Stop Single App Mode.

Settings still work inside Single App Mode. The PIN-protected settings are part of the app, so you can change the room or the look from the wall without unlocking the device.

Part 6: mount it

  • Power is non-negotiable. A mount with cable routing, or a cable to a nearby outlet.
  • Settings › Display & Brightness › Auto-Lock › Never, as a belt-and-braces (the app disables the idle timer anyway, and Single App Mode's Auto-Lock option covers it).
  • Landscape is the usual orientation for a door sign; the board handles either.

Ongoing

  • App updates: supervised iPads with no Apple ID don't auto-update apps from the App Store. Reconnect to Configurator › Update, or move to an MDM when the count justifies it.
  • Adding a room: share it with the service account (Part 1, step 3). The next iPad you set up sees it; existing iPads see it under Settings › Calendar.
  • Rotating the key: Cloud Console › the service account › Keys › add a new key, delete the old one. Each iPad: hold the corner › PIN › Settings › Remove service account, then load the new file.

If something's off

  • Configurator can't see my Business Manager apps. The organization isn't signed in (Settings › Organizations), or the licenses are assigned to a different location in Apps and Books.
  • "Prepare" fails partway. Usually the cable or a USB hub. Use a direct port and Apple's cable.
  • No rooms appear in the app. Part 1, steps 2 and 3. The admin sharing setting takes a few minutes to apply.
  • The board is right but Single App Mode won't start. The iPad must be supervised by this Mac's organization. If it was supervised elsewhere, re-prepare it.
