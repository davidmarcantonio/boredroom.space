Privacy policy

Last updated November 14, 2026. Applies to the Bored Room app for iPad and iPhone and to boredroom.space.

Bored Room is made by BACS Tech, Inc. ("BACS Tech", "we", "us"). This policy explains what the app and this website do with your data, which is as little as possible.

Bored Room does not collect, store, transmit, or sell any personal data. There is no Bored Room server. The app runs on your iPad and talks only to Google, plus, if your organization sets one, the background image address it chose. If you set the iPad up with your iPhone, the two talk to each other directly and nothing else is involved.

What the app does with your data

The app reads the room's calendar in one of two ways, both chosen by you. With Google sign-in, the app asks for two calendar permissions (the full list is below): to see the list of calendars the account is subscribed to, so you can pick the room, and to read and create events, so the board can show the room's meetings and the room can be booked from the iPad. With a service account, you load a key file your administrator created; the iPad uses it to read only the calendars your administrator shared with that account, and the key never leaves the iPad.

Calendar data is fetched from Google's servers by the iPad and displayed on the iPad. It is not sent anywhere else. It is not written to disk beyond iPadOS's normal caching. It is refreshed every minute and replaced.

Your credential, whether a Google sign-in token or a service account key, is stored in the iPad's keychain, protected by the device. It is used only to talk to Google. Removing it in the app deletes it from the iPad; a sign-in token is also revoked with Google.

Setting up with an iPhone

The iPhone version of Bored Room does one thing: set up and change iPads. It holds no calendar and no sign-in.

  • A direct, encrypted connection. The iPhone and the iPad connect to each other over the local network or directly device to device, never through the internet or a server. The connection is encrypted with a one-time key that exists only in the code the iPad shows. The key is made fresh each time and stops working when the iPad closes that screen.
  • The iPhone never keeps the Google sign-in. You sign in on the iPhone, but Google hands the iPhone only a one-time code, which the iPhone passes to the iPad. Only that iPad can exchange the code for a sign-in (Google's PKCE protection), and the iPad stores it in its keychain, as described above. The iPhone signs in privately, so the account doesn't stay signed in on the phone afterward.
  • What crosses the connection: that one-time code; from the iPad, the signed-in account's email address, the list of rooms it can see, and the iPad's current room, name, hours and booking setting, so the iPhone can show them; and from the iPhone, your answers (room, name, PIN, hours, booking). The iPhone shows these only on screen while you set up and keeps none of them. Everything is stored on the iPad only.
  • The camera is used only to read the code on the iPad's screen. No photo is taken or kept.
  • Local Network permission is what iPadOS and iOS ask for before two devices can find each other. Bored Room uses it only during setup.

What stays on the iPad

  • The room name, chosen calendar, theme, font, and display preferences.
  • A background photo, if you chose one. The app keeps a resized copy in its own storage.
  • A hashed copy of your settings PIN.

Deleting the app deletes all of it, except credentials in the keychain, which are cleared when you remove them in the app. A Google sign-in can also be revoked at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

What we don't do

  • No analytics or usage tracking of any kind.
  • No crash reporting services.
  • No advertising, no advertising identifiers.
  • No third-party SDKs. The app contains only our code and Apple's frameworks.
  • No network connections except to Google's OAuth and Calendar APIs; during setup with an iPhone, the direct connection between your iPhone and iPad; and, only when your organization's MDM sets a background image address, a plain download of that image.
  • No logging of meeting titles, attendees, or any calendar content, on the device or elsewhere.

What Apple may tell us

If you have opted in to sharing analytics with developers in iPadOS settings, Apple provides us with aggregate, anonymous statistics such as device model, OS version, and country. We cannot see individual devices or users. We use this to decide which iPads and OS versions to support. You can turn this off under Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.

Google API Services

Bored Room's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data obtained through Google APIs is used only to display and, when you choose, book the selected room's calendar on the iPad, and, during setup with an iPhone, to show you the account's room list on your own iPhone. It is never transferred to others, used for advertising, or read by humans.

This website

boredroom.space is a static site. It sets no cookies and runs no analytics. If you email us, we keep the email so we can reply.

Managed deployments

If your organization deploys Bored Room through an MDM, the organization may push settings (service account key, room name, calendar or list of rooms, PIN, theme, hours, background image address) to the iPad. Those settings are handled by your organization's MDM under its own privacy terms; Bored Room only reads them from the device. If a background image address is set, the iPad downloads that image about once a day with a plain request: no cookies, no account, and nothing about the room or its calendar. Like any web request, it reveals the network's IP address to whoever runs that server, which is your organization or a host it chose.

Children

Bored Room is a business tool and is not directed at children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes and the previous version is kept in this website's public history on GitHub. We will not change the central promise: no backend, no data collection.
