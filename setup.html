Set up Bored Room

Pick the guide that matches how you manage iPads. Each one starts with a "Before you start" section and a checklist (accounts, access, downloads), then the hands-on part. They print cleanly, so you can hand the prerequisites to the Google admin and the rest to whoever's holding the iPad.

Start here: the wall comes before the app. The app takes five minutes. Getting power to an iPad next to a conference room door can take weeks, and it can cost far more than 99¢. Before you set anything up (or buy iPads), work through this in order:

  1. Pick the spot next to each door and measure it.
  2. Check for an outlet nearby, or plan an Ethernet cable that powers the iPad (power over Ethernet).
  3. Buy one mount and hold it in place to make sure it fits.
  4. If you need wiring, get an electrician or cabling installer to spec it and quote it, and get the quote approved.
  5. Mount it, power it, then set up the app.

Mounting and power walks through each step. The checklist has a sheet to print for each room and one for the office.

Then pick your guide

Set up with iPhone

Easiest. For anyone with an iPhone and the room's Google account

Open Bored Room on the iPad and tap "Set up with iPhone". Scan the code it shows with Bored Room on your iPhone, connect Google Calendar on the phone, and answer a few questions there. The iPad keeps the sign-in; your iPhone doesn't. Works on guest Wi-Fi, or with no shared network at all.

Your iPhone may need its own copy (99¢). See "Do you need Bored Room on your iPhone?" below.

About 5 minutes per iPad, once the room is on your Google Calendar

By hand

For an office admin, no IT background needed

A few rooms. Add the room to your Google Calendar, connect it on the iPad with the same account, pick the room, hang it up. No Google Cloud, no admin settings beyond creating the room.

About 15 minutes the first time, 5 per iPad after

With Apple Configurator

For the one-person IT shop

Supervise the iPads from a Mac, install the app through Apple Business, and lock each one to the board so it survives reboots.

About 30 minutes, then 5 per iPad

Through your MDM

For IT teams with Jamf, Mosyle, Iru (Kandji), Intune, or similar

Push the app, the kiosk lock, and every setting including the room and the credential. iPads go from the box to the board untouched, or one profile covers every room and whoever mounts the iPad picks it.

About 30 minutes, then zero per iPad

Do you need Bored Room on your iPhone?

For offices setting up iPads by hand, without an MDM. On an iPhone, Bored Room doesn't show a room board. It's a setup remote for your iPads, so it's never required. Whether it's worth it depends on how many iPads you have.

What the iPhone app does
  1. The iPad shows a code. You scan it with Bored Room on your iPhone.
  2. You sign in to Google and pick the room, name, PIN and hours on your phone, not on the wall.
  3. The iPad gets the settings and keeps the sign-in. Your phone keeps nothing.
  4. Later, Settings › Room › "Change from iPhone" moves an iPad to another room in about a minute.

How did the iPads get Bored Room?

Bought in the App Store, with the same Apple Account your iPhone uses
Free

One purchase covers your iPhone and iPad. On your iPhone, the App Store shows a download (cloud) icon instead of 99¢.

Through Apple Business or Apple Configurator, or someone else bought them
99¢

Those copies belong to each iPad, not to you, so your phone needs its own. Buy it once and it sets up every iPad in the office.

Using Guided Access or Single App Mode? It works on a locked iPad too. The first time, both devices ask for permission to find devices on the local network; tap Allow on each.

Have an MDM? You don't need this. The MDM guide covers setup.

Two ways to connect to Google. By hand, the iPad connects to a Google account that has the room on its calendar, typed on the iPad or passed over from your iPhone. Apple Configurator can do the same with a shared account like rooms@. Zero-touch MDM uses a free Google service account, so no one's account lives on the iPad; it means loosening two Google settings, which that guide lists up front. If you'd rather do it on a call, book a free setup session.
