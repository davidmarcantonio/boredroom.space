Set up Bored Room

Pick the guide that matches how you manage iPads. Each one starts with a "Before you start" section and a checklist (accounts, access, downloads), then the hands-on part. They print cleanly, so you can hand the prerequisites to the Google admin and the rest to whoever's holding the iPad.

Start here: the wall comes before the app. The app takes five minutes. Getting power to an iPad next to a conference room door can take weeks, and it can cost far more than 99¢. Before you set anything up (or buy iPads), work through this in order:

  1. Pick the spot next to each door and measure it.
  2. Check for an outlet nearby, or plan an Ethernet cable that powers the iPad (power over Ethernet).
  3. Buy one mount and hold it in place to make sure it fits.
  4. If you need wiring, get an electrician or cabling installer to spec it and quote it, and get the quote approved.
  5. Mount it, power it, then set up the app.

Mounting and power walks through each step. The checklist has a sheet to print for each room and one for the office.

Then pick your guide

By hand

For an office admin, no IT background needed

A few rooms. Add the room to your Google Calendar, sign in on the iPad with the same account, pick the room, hang it up. No Google Cloud, no admin settings beyond creating the room.

About 15 minutes the first time, 5 per iPad after

With Apple Configurator

For the one-person IT shop

Supervise the iPads from a Mac, install the app through Apple Business, and lock each one to the board so it survives reboots.

About 30 minutes, then 5 per iPad

Through your MDM

For IT teams with Jamf, Mosyle, Iru (Kandji), Intune, or similar

Push the app, the kiosk lock, and every setting including the room and the credential. iPads go from the box to the board untouched.

About 30 minutes, then zero per iPad

Two ways to connect to Google. By hand, the iPad signs in with a Google account that has the room on its calendar. Apple Configurator can do the same with a shared account like rooms@. Zero-touch MDM uses a free Google service account, so no one's account lives on the iPad; it means loosening two Google settings, which that guide lists up front.
