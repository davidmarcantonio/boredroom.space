Built for the office with three rooms, not three thousand.

Room-booking platforms are priced and designed for enterprises. Most offices just want a sign on the door that's right. This is that.

No backend

The iPad talks to Google Calendar directly. There's no Bored Room server in between, so there's nothing to go down, nothing to subscribe to, and nothing that sees your calendar but you and Google.

No accounts

You never create an account with us. The app uses the Google Workspace account you sign in with on the iPad and nothing else.

No data collected

We don't run analytics, we don't log meetings, we don't phone home. The App Store privacy label says "Data Not Collected" because it's true.

How it works

  1. Buy it

    $1 on the App Store, or through Apple Business Manager if you deploy iPads in bulk. One copy per iPad.

  2. Sign in and pick the room

    Open the app, sign in with a Google Workspace account that can see the room, choose the room from the list, set a PIN. About a minute.

  3. Hang it up

    Lock the iPad to the app with Guided Access or Single App Mode. The screen stays on, the calendar refreshes every minute, and walk-ins can book the room from the door.

Privacy is the product.

A room sign sees every meeting in that room. The only responsible design is one where that information never leaves the building.

  • We never see your calendar. The iPad reads it from Google and shows it. That's the entire data path.
  • Private meetings show as "Private meeting". Turn off titles entirely and every meeting shows as "Meeting".
  • Your Google sign-in is stored on the iPad's own keychain, nowhere else. Sign out and it's gone.
  • No analytics, no crash reporting, no tracking, no third-party code. The app makes network requests to Google's calendar API and to nothing else.
  • Apple may share anonymous device and OS statistics with us, only if you've opted in on the device. That's the only "metric" we'll ever have.

Why we can say this. There is no Bored Room backend. We couldn't collect your data if we wanted to, and we've designed it so we never want to. Read the full privacy policy. It's short.

Questions

Which iPads does it run on?

Any iPad on iPadOS 17 or later. An older iPad you already own is fine; the app is light. It works in landscape and portrait.

Does it work with Microsoft 365 or Outlook?

Not yet. Version 1 is Google Workspace only. Microsoft 365 is next; if that's you, tell us so we know how many of you there are.

What does the app need from Google?

Read access to the room's calendar, and, if you turn on booking from the door, permission to create events. It reads one calendar: the room you picked. Workspace admins can also see the full room list during setup.

Why does Google say the app is "unverified"?

Google reviews apps that ask for calendar access, and the review takes weeks. Until it's done you'll see a warning screen on first sign-in; tap through it. Workspace admins can also mark Bored Room as trusted for their organization in the Admin console.

How do I keep people from changing the settings?

Settings sit behind a PIN you set during setup. For a wall-mounted iPad, lock it to the app with Guided Access (built into iPadOS) or Single App Mode via Apple Configurator or your MDM. The guide shows both.

Is there a subscription, ever?

No. $1 buys the app. Updates are free. There's no server to pay for, so there's nothing to charge you for.
