Built for the office with three rooms, not three thousand.

Room-booking platforms are priced and designed for enterprises. Most offices just want a sign on the door that's right. This is that.

No backend

The iPad talks to Google Calendar directly. There's no Bored Room server in between, so there's nothing to go down, nothing to subscribe to, and nothing that sees your calendar but you and Google.

No extra licenses

Sign the iPad in with a Google account you already have. If IT would rather nobody's account lived on the wall, a Google service account does the same job for free. Either way you don't buy a Workspace seat for a room.

No data collected

We don't run analytics, we don't log meetings, we don't phone home. The App Store privacy label says "Data Not Collected" because it's true.

How it works

  1. Buy it

    99¢ on the App Store, or through Apple Business Manager if you deploy iPads in bulk. One copy per iPad.

  2. Sign in, pick the room

    Add the room to your Google Calendar, sign in on the iPad with the same account, pick the room, set a PIN. IT teams can use a free Google service account instead, and push everything from an MDM.

  3. Hang it up

    Lock the iPad to the app with Guided Access or Single App Mode. The screen stays on, the calendar refreshes every minute, and walk-ins can book the room from the door.

Privacy is the product.

A room sign sees every meeting in that room. The only responsible design is one where that information never leaves the building.

  • We never see your calendar. The iPad reads it from Google and shows it. That's the entire data path.
  • Private meetings show as "Private meeting". Turn off titles entirely and every meeting shows as "Meeting".
  • Your Google sign-in is stored on the iPad's own keychain, nowhere else. Sign out and it's gone.
  • No analytics, no crash reporting, no tracking, no third-party code. The app makes network requests to Google's calendar API and to nothing else (except a background image, if your IT team sets one in its MDM).
  • Apple may share anonymous device and OS statistics with us, only if you've opted in on the device. That's the only "metric" we'll ever have.

Why we can say this. There is no Bored Room backend. We couldn't collect your data if we wanted to, and we've designed it so we never want to. Read the full privacy policy. It's short.

The room-display platforms charge extra for "enterprise deployment." Bored Room reads Apple's Managed App Configuration out of the box, so Jamf, Mosyle, Iru (formerly Kandji), Intune, or any other MDM can configure every iPad before it's even unboxed. Ten keys, all optional.

What an admin can set from the MDM

  • Service account key: the iPad reads the room with no sign-in at all.
  • Room calendar: which Google room this iPad shows.
  • Room name: what's printed at the top of the board.
  • Settings PIN: the 4 digits that protect the settings.
  • Booking from the door: on or off.
  • Meeting titles: show them, or show "Meeting."
  • Background and font: pick from the built-in sets.
  • Hours: which days and hours the board is lit, or always on.

What that means in practice

Anything set from the MDM is locked on the device, so nobody at the door can change it. With the key, calendar, name, and PIN pushed, an iPad boots straight to the board; add a Single App Mode payload and it stays there.

Questions

Which iPads does it run on?

Any iPad on iPadOS 17 or later. An older iPad you already own is fine; the app is light. It works in landscape and portrait.

Does it work with Microsoft 365 or Outlook?

Not yet. Version 1 is Google Workspace only. Microsoft 365 is next; if that's you, tell us so we know how many of you there are.

Do I need to buy a Google Workspace account for the room?

No. The simplest setup signs the iPad in with an account you already have, ideally a shared one like front desk. If you'd rather no one's account were on the iPad, a Google service account is free, has no mailbox, and reads only the rooms you share with it. The setup guides cover both.

What does the app need from Google?

Two permissions: the list of calendars on the account, so you can pick the room, and access to events, so it can show the room's meetings and let people book it from the door. Once set up, it reads one calendar: the room you picked.

Why does Google say the app is "unverified" when I sign in?

Google reviews every app that asks for calendar access, and the review takes weeks. Until it's done, Google shows a warning at sign-in: tap Advanced, then continue to Bored Room. If your organization blocks unreviewed apps, your Google admin can mark Bored Room as trusted; the by-hand guide shows where. Service accounts never see this screen.

What happens at night and on weekends?

During setup you pick the days and hours the board should be on (Monday to Friday, 7 AM to 5 PM by default, or always on). Outside those hours the screen goes black and the backlight drops to its minimum, which is as close to off as an iPad app can get. Anyone who walks up and touches it gets the live board for a minute. A meeting that runs late keeps the board lit until it ends. In the morning it comes back on its own; nobody has to press anything. For OLED iPad Pros the board also shifts its layout by a few pixels every few minutes, so nothing sits on the same pixels for hours.

I forgot the PIN.

Tap "Forgot PIN?" on the PIN screen. You prove you're the admin the same way the iPad was set up: sign in again as the board's Google account, or choose the service account's key file again. Then pick a new PIN. The iPad stays on the wall. If your MDM pushes the PIN, change it there instead. In a small setup, deleting and reinstalling the app also clears the PIN.

How do I keep people from changing the settings?

Settings sit behind a PIN you set during setup. For a wall-mounted iPad, lock it to the app with Guided Access (built into iPadOS) or Single App Mode via Apple Configurator or your MDM. The guide shows both.

Is there a subscription, ever?

No. 99¢ buys the app. Updates are free. There's no server to pay for, so there's nothing to charge you for.
